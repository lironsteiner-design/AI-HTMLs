K KATE prompt playbook
Bi-weekly lesson learned

Turn past project knowledge into usable insight.

Use KATE to search past work for examples, risks, people, and constructability lessons. Start with the outcome you need, then add enough scope to keep the search focused.

A strong starting point

Action + topic + scope + desired output

Prompt patterns

Choose the pattern that matches the question you are trying to answer.

01

Find examples

Use precedent to ground a new calculation or decision.

“Show me timber falsework calc packages from the past few years and list the designer, EOR, and state.”
02

Find similar tasks

Use comparable projects to prepare for work that is just starting.

“Using the five most similar past projects, prepare a design kickoff brief for a new temporary working surface. Include likely load cases, coordination questions, and risks to consider.”
03

Find experts

Locate people with relevant project or role experience.

“Who has worked on high-risk bridge demos as the EOR or designer?”
04

Test assumptions

Pressure-test a design assumption against past practice.

“I’m assuming a uniform load for [topic]. Is there an example of this being done in a trestle design?”
05

Understand constructability

Learn how design intent changed during detailing and field installation.

“Find projects where the temporary works design changed between the calculation package, shop drawings, and field installation. Summarize the changes and why they occurred.”
06

Surface common risks

Ask for recurring risk themes in project records and review forms.

“For falsework systems, what are the key risks generally identified in the QRSD form within the QC folder?”

Speed up or narrow the search

Add one or two constraints when the first answer is too broad.

Search in [X] files Within the past [X] years In [state / region] Give [X] examples Only relevant tasks Limit the search time Specify folder / file type
Keep prompts specific enough to guide the search, open enough to reveal useful precedent. Images intentionally omitted.
